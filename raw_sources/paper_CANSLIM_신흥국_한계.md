Abstract
There are so many alternative asset securities in capital market, investor must chose 
some of them witch have a good performance to maximize their portfolio return. One of several 
methods to choose asset securities that have a good performance is CAN SLIM. CAN SLIM 
analyze securities depends on: Current Quarterly Earning Per Shares, Annual Earning 
Increases, New Product, New Management and New High, Supply and Demand Leader, 
Laggard, and Market Direction. We compare return of portfolio that build by CAN SLIM with 
market return. 
Results for the selection method is at stake CAN SLIM a consistent entry in the list LQ45 
Index from February 2009 to January 2010 is comprised of six stocks, namely: ELSA, INTP, 
ITMG, PTBA, SMGR and UNTR. And then to test the performance of CAN SLIM stocks in 
generating positive return, then the return CAN SLIM choices stock which has been established 
portfolio is compared with market return in 2010 as a benchmark from the movement of LQ45 in 
the year 2010. The results with independent t-test for two sample means indicate that the CAN 
SLIM stock portfolio has not better performance in generating return compared with market 
return, both on daily, weekly and monthly trade data. 
Keywords: securities performance, market performance, return.

Fundamental analysis is a method used to determine the value of a stock by analyzing the 
financial data essential to the company with the goal of projecting future price movements. 
Investors examine the company’s financial and operations by looking at sales, earnings, 
dividends, growth potential, assets, debt, management, products and competition, in order to 
verify if a stock should be bought or sold. At the other side, technical analysis considers the past 
price movements of a stock to determine its future actions. Technical analysis is often described 
as an art which requires experience, skills and dedication in order to be mastered. CAN SLIM combine fundamental and technical analysis by adding value to the 
investment methodology through the concept of complete trading strategies. CAN SLIM is a 
checklist for seven common characteristics all great performing stocks have before their major 
price increase. CAN SLIM analyze securities depend on: Current Quarterly Earning Per Shares, 
Annual Earning Increases, New Product, New Management and New High, Supply and Demand 
Leader, Laggard, and Market Direction.

2. Study Literature
There are two different fundamental analysis methodologies. Value investors assign a fair 
value to stocks by using a methodology that was developed by Graham and Dodd (Value 
Investing). If this fair value is not equal to the actual market price of the stock, fundamental 
analysts believe that the stock is under or overvalued and that it will settle towards the fair value. 
On the other hand, growth investors buy stocks at any given price and hope to sell it even higher. 
Although a stock might have a high price, indicated by a high price to earnings ratio, investors 
purchase the stock because of its potential high growth. Growth investing is considered riskier; 
however it is appealing to investors because of the high returns it promises.
The CAN SLIM approach seeks companies with a proven record of quarterly and annual 
earnings and sales growth showing strong relative price strength and support from leading 
institutions. O’Neil (2002) does not mind paying rich premiums for stocks with good prospects. 
He feels that most strategies seeking stocks with low price-earnings are flawed because they 
ignore the price trend determining the price-earnings ratio, as well as the quality of the 
underlying earnings within the ratio. O’Neil (2002) believes that stocks generally sell for what 
they are worth and most stocks with low price-earnings ratios are probably priced correctly by 
the market. O’Neil also asserts that it is important to follow the market closely and try to lighten 
up your stock exposure when going into a bear market.
Quantitative analysis is the biggest part of fundamental analysis. This engages looking at 
past data to forecast future earnings. Some of the variables used for fundamental analysis that are 
relevant to CAN SLIM Stocks are explained below.
2.1 Current Quarterly Earning per Share (EPS)
EPS is earnings per share are calculated by dividing net incomeby the number of stock sold by 
the company. EPS can demonstrate the ability a stock to earnings income. The higher earning per 
EPS is better. The rules of CAN SLIM method we should choose company that have 18% 
increases in quarterly EPS compared to last year in a same quarter. Besides, the sales should be 
increases for 25 % along the increases of quarterly EPS, because an increase of earning if not 
followed by sales should not represent the actual earning. 2.2 Annual Earning Increases 
Increases of the last three-quarter combine with annual earning increases in the last three years 
3. Result
Assumptions in the calculation of the portfolio in this study is the calculation of portfolio 
returns based on stocks that have been on the fundamental criteria CAN SLIM. The reason, 
technical analysis fluctuated during the study period so it is difficult to calculate and set the 
calculation period. The second assumption, stocks are assumed to be formed portfolio held by 
the investor on January 4 to December 23, 2010 with the same weight is considered large. 
This study begins with the determination of the sample. The company that made as the 
initial sample are firms that are included in the LQ-45 index during two periods, the period from 
February to July 2009 and August 2009-January 2010 an there was 38 samples that selected for 
this initial selection process. After 38 samples selected we use CAN SLIM method as a guideline
for the next eliminating process. CAN SLIM methods provide us to selecting the 38 stocks to 
only 6 stocks that represent the criteria required. The 6 stocks are : ELSA, INTP, ITMG, PTBA, 
SMGR and UNTR. After having those stocks we built a portfolio consist from selected stocks 
we assume the weighted of portfolio are same because we try to reduce a manipulation on a 
return of portfolio. Realized stock return calculated with daily stock trading data, weekly and 
monthly. Calculation of the average stock return realized by using the arithmetic mean was 
conducted during the year 2010, starting on January 4, 2010 as the base date, until December 23,

5. Conclusion
Comparative return of a portfolio of stocks selected based on fundamental criteria CAN 
SLIM with market return, according to the results of statistical tests independent t - test for two 
sample means as in the hypothesis testing shows that the whole calculation, whether by trade 
data daily, weekly or monthly in year 2010 indicates that the shares do not have a portfolio return 
that is greater than the market return. This illustrates the performance of a stock portfolio CAN 
fundamental choice in producing return CAN SLIM in 2010, has not been able to meet the 
expectations of the market because it is not able to exceed the average market return in 2010. 
CAN SLIM choose their inability in shares in excess of the average market due to several 
weaknesses can be identified through the process and results of this study:
6. CAN SLIM method does not differentiate the treatment of shares with different financial 
structures. Different financial structures owned shares in financial institutions, such as 
banking, insurance, and so on
7. CAN SLIM method can not find the stocks that the average return well above market 
return, resulting portfolio returns were not statistically better than the market return
8. In stock selection process CAN SLIM, particularly at the level of Current Quarterly EPS 
assess good stocks to choose from is that a 18% increase in EPS over, to no avail 
accommodate for stocks with a small value of early EPS will have a larger percentage 
increase if compared with the value of shares of enterprises with large EPS in the initial 
period. 
9. CAN SLIM method can not answer the weighting problem. Weighting is an important 
problem in the formation of a stock portfolio. Earned profit largely depends strongly on 
proportion of funds provided to each stock
